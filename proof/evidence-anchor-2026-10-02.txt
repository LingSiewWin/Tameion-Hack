# Evidence desk: a pack's hash anchored on Arc, and four ways to fail verification — 2026-10-02

`scripts/evidence/anchor.py` (new). `pack.py` stays read-only; this is the one
step that sends a transaction: the pack's sha256 to MemoryAnchor
0xDAb65A5380BCC2Eebc3A3A91d0AD11Eec95994dB on Arc testnet, `anchor(bytes32)`.
Packs now carry a random 32-byte salt inside the hash, so the published hash
cannot be used to confirm a guess about whose pack it is. Tests: 20 offline
(salt, tamper detection, receipt parsing: wrong status, contract, root, event).

The pack: the same public Arbitrum DAO treasury as the 10-01 demo
(0x897b41bdf47ef039484cacbbd2e07f88899f1f96, 2026-01-01 → 2026-10-01).
Personal data rules apply to real earners' packs; this one is a public treasury.

## A copy, edited three ways

1. One amount multiplied by ten:
runtime/evidence/demo-tampered/pack.json does not match its own sha256: it was edited after it was written   (exit 1)

2. …and the stated sha256 recomputed to match the edit:
record matches its sha256 405e1520f0846f3d…
ANCHOR MISMATCH: pack.anchor.json is for 573a88ab89e9e5ac…, not this record                                   (exit 1)

3. …and pack.anchor.json rewritten to claim the new hash:
record matches its sha256 405e1520f0846f3d…
ANCHOR NOT FOUND: 0x242c7890… does not anchor this record at 0xDAb65A53…                                       (exit 1)

What this proves, and does not: the record existed exactly as it is no later
than block 64988496. It does not prove the record is right -- each line still
has its own explorer link to check -- and on a testnet it proves nothing to a
bank yet. The same call works on any chain with a MemoryAnchor.
