# Clear signing: the page shows the call, computes its challenge, signs only that — 2026-10-02

Before: web/passkey/index.html signed whatever 32-byte challenge it was given.
A relayer -- the party that holds no authority by design -- could hand the
owner a challenge for "allow 0xAttacker" and the owner would approve a hash
they could not read. Now a link carries the call (?owner=&chain=&n=&to=&data=),
the page decodes it into words, computes PasskeyPrincipal.challengeFor itself
(web/passkey/call.js: Keccak-256 + ABI encoding, no dependencies), and signs
that. A bare challenge is labelled a blind approval; an unrecognised call
disables the button.

## Found on the way
call.js first declared its helpers at top level; loaded as a classic script
next to the page's own, `const hex` collided ("Identifier 'hex' has already
been declared") and the page's whole script stopped -- the clear-signing block
never ran and the page silently fell back to the blind input. call.js now
lives in one function scope and exports only TameionCall.
